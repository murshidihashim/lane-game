## SUMMARY

A playable one-on-one match against a computer opponent, in portrait, in the phone browser.
There are three lanes; the middle one is long and winding. Each side has one tower per lane and
a Core. Two jungle camps sit on the river, and minion waves come every 20 seconds.
- **Cards:** one fixed 8-card deck (6 troops/spells and 2 heroes), with 4 in hand. You drag a
  card, or tap it and then tap a lane. Mana refills by itself, twice as fast in the last minute.
- **Heroes:** Ranger and Warden stay all match, level 1–4 and come back after dying.
  - The owner drags a hero's portrait onto the map to give one of five orders: push, defend,
    gank, farm or retreat.
  - A hero in the jungle is hidden from the other side, and its first hit after that is an
    Ambush (double damage).
- **Settings:** 2 or 3 lanes, speed (1×, 1.5×, 2×) and bot level (easy or normal).
- **Ending:** a result screen with a Rematch button.

## PLAN OR WORK COMPLETED

- **Rules (`src/game/`):**
  - No graphics code; whole-number maths only; seeded random numbers.
  - A replay is the seed plus the list of moves, which is the base for online play later.
- **Computer opponent (`src/game/bot.ts`):** follows simple rules:
  - Defends the lane under the most pressure.
  - Fires Firebolt at groups of troops.
  - Sends a Ram with support.
  - Gives its heroes orders, including ganks.
  - Respects hidden heroes.
- **Screen (`src/ui/`):**
  - Canvas drawing sized for a phone.
  - Drop zones and tower ranges show while you place a card.
  - Order targets show while you drag a hero.
  - "?" marks where a hidden enemy hero was last seen.
- **Tools:** TypeScript, Vite (build) and Vitest (tests), all dev-only, with no game engine
  (decided in the plan). The build makes one self-contained 59 KB page.

## NOT DONE OR NOT VERIFIED

- **Balance is untested with people:**
  - In 100 bot-vs-bot matches: blue won 22, red won 22 and 56 were draws.
  - 14 matches ended with a Core destroyed, and the average match lasted 322 s.
  - The high draw rate shows the two bots cancel each other out. That isn't proof of how human
    matches go.
