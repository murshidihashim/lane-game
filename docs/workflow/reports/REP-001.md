## SUMMARY

- **What the research found:**
  - No real-time card game with three contested lanes has found a mass audience yet.
  - The games that worked kept the player's load small: two lanes, one resource and short matches
    in Clash Royale's case.
  - Failed attempts each added something extra: a hero you steer by hand, a busy third lane, a
    second economy, a large campaign, or paid power.
- **Market:** Southeast Asia knows MOBAs well but spends about US$19 per player a year. Malaysia
  spends more than its size suggests and has a high share of iPhones.
- **Legal:** game mechanics can be borrowed, but names, art, maps and screen layouts cannot. Paid
  random rewards face tightening rules. This is general research, not legal advice.
- **Full report:** `docs/research/research-report.md`. Many sources were blocked during the
  research, so its figures are estimates.

## PLAN OR WORK COMPLETED

- Research report and notes (`docs/research/`).
- Concept and screen mockup (`docs/design/`).
- Decisions, all approved with the owner's own words:
  - DEC-001: portrait
  - DEC-002: hero cards with orders (push, defend, gank, farm, retreat)
  - DEC-003: longer, winding middle lane that keeps the map symmetrical
  - DEC-004: hero abilities fire automatically
  - DEC-005: this repository
- PLAN-001 "Grey-box prototype vs bot" (proposed): a phone-browser prototype with simple shapes, a
  2-lane / 3-lane switch, two heroes, five orders and a bot opponent. Its goal is to find out whether
  three lanes are readable on a phone and whether hero orders are fun on top of cards.
